How it plays
Old Maid, crew rules.

The deck. Three queens come out — spades, diamonds, clubs. The Queen of Hearts stays in with nobody to match her. That leaves 49 cards: 24 pairs and her.

The deal. All 49 go out one at a time, starting left of the dealer. Hands come out uneven — that’s the game, not a bug. Everyone throws out the pairs they were dealt.

The play. The dealer fans their hand face down to the player on their left, who takes one blind. Pair it, bin it. That player then offers their own hand to their left, and round it goes. Empty your hand and you’re out, safe.

The end. Last one holding the Queen of Hearts is stuck with her, and whatever that costs at your table is between you and them.

Where the skill is

Drawing is blind, so the game is won and lost on tells — the same as at a real table. When somebody offers you their fan, watch for a card that twitches. That’s their nerves showing. The better the player, the less they leak, and the sharp ones will fake a twitch to send you the wrong way.

You leak too. Leaving the Queen on either end of your fan and offering it up unshuffled is the oldest tell there is, and so is sitting there thinking about it. Sort your hand to find pairs fast, then shuffle before you offer — a sorted fan is a readable fan.

The five levels
1 DaftNever shuffles, shoves the Queen to one end, and telegraphs it nearly every time.
2 SlackShuffles now and again. Still leaks about half the time.
3 RegularShuffles most offers, the odd tell, will occasionally bluff one.
4 SharpShuffles every time, barely leaks, bluffs, and notices when you do.
5 SharkNever leaks. Reads your habits and plants the Queen exactly where you keep reaching.
